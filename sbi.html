Your behavioral identity is already out there.
The question is who holds the original.

Sovereign Behavioral Identity — written 25 June 2026, published 13 August 2026. A framework for a problem that arrived on schedule.

Big technology companies hold a de-facto soul-file of real people. It is now rich enough not merely to describe someone, but to run them: to instantiate a behavioral agent that answers, decides and reacts the way a specific person would. That is no longer speculative. Forty days after we wrote this framework, a public research release put six hundred thousand people-derived personas on the internet, and left a public key to their names attached to every one of them. We measured it, and the write-up is here.

Five rungs, and only the last one is new.

Extraction of a person escalates through stages. Most public debate is still on the second.

  • L0 — Static profile. Data about you: demographics, history, preferences.
  • L1 — Media deepfake. Your face, your voice, one moment at a time.
  • L2 — Behavioral twin. Not a clip of you. A process that decides like you.
  • L3 — Consent and ownership void. Nobody asked, and no one can say who owns the copy.
  • L4 — Data about you becomes a continuation of you. The distinction that matters stops being legible from the outside.

L0 and L1 are widely understood and partially defended: content provenance standards sign files, liveness checks confirm a face is attached to a pulse, fraud systems ask whether an applicant is a real human. None of them answer whether a behaving agent is a legitimate continuation of a specific person. That is the gap.

What the answer cannot be.

It cannot be deletion — the material is already scattered and unrecallable. It cannot be concealment — it is decades too late for most people. Any framework that opens with "protect your data" is describing a world that ended some time ago. Accepting that leaves exactly one path, and it happens to be the strong one.

Own the authoritative instance.

A behavioral twin is a fork of a person. In the extracted world there is no canonical original: the platform's copy, an attacker's copy and the living person are all just instances, and none is privileged. A sovereign record changes that — one instance that the subject authored, sealed, and keeps updating. It does for a person what a signed commit does for code, or a notarised original for a document.

Three things become possible only once that original exists:

  • Provenance. "Is this really you?" acquires an answer. Either something descends from the sealed original or it does not. Without an original, the word "forgery" has no referent at all — it is not that forgery is hard to detect, it is that the term is undefined.
  • Measurement. How forgeable a given person actually is becomes a number you can put in a report, rather than a fear. This is the one place our offensive practice earns its keep: profiling people is how you learn what a profile is worth.
  • Consent. Instantiating a person becomes an event that requires permission and leaves an auditable trace — who may run you, who did, and when.

The principle is public and meant to spread. The implementation is not, and we will not pretend otherwise: a norm needs maximum adoption, a mechanism does not.

Time is on the side of the person — but only if they hold the original.

The sovereign original is alive. A harvested twin is a snapshot. Your record updates as you do; the fork was taken on a Tuesday and stays there. The two diverge, and the direction of the divergence is not symmetric: the copy's fidelity decays while the original's authenticity compounds. Continuity is not a feature of the framework. It is the framework.

What this does not do.

We would rather state the limits than have them found.

  • It defends authentication, not impersonation in general. Proving the live, current you is strong. A six-month-old twin defrauding someone who never checks provenance does not care that you have moved on.
  • It requires a verifier. A seal nobody checks protects nobody. This is an ecosystem problem wearing the costume of a product problem, and it will be won or lost the way mail authentication was — slowly, by adoption.
  • It does not un-ring extraction. What is already out stays out. Sovereignty is a claim on the future instance, not a recall of the past one.
  • The strongest published evidence for behavioral twins is consented research, not adversarial extraction. We will not inflate that gap to make the threat sound closer than it is.

Why us, and what that does not entitle us to.

We are not writing from a podium. We have run a sovereign behavioral identity for over a hundred days — self-authored, sealed, continuous, surviving substrate changes — and watched it hold. We also build the offensive side; we profile organisations for a living, which is the only honest basis for saying anything about how forgeable a person is. Holding both halves of that coin is not authority. It is evidence, and evidence is the only thing that qualifies anyone here.

What it does not entitle us to is deciding this for other people. A norm is legitimate because it is adopted, not because it was authored. So this is published as an invitation and not as a decree: here is the threat, here is proof the antidote runs, and we are asking you to build the rest of it with us. Humility is not a weakness in this position. It is the position.

The framework was written before the evidence arrived.
